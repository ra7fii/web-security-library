# Reading list

Commercial books worth buying. **None of them are hosted in this repository**; each entry links to the publisher or author. No Starch Press sells DRM-free PDF, EPUB and MOBI copies, and its print books come with the ebook.

Levels: 🟢 beginner · 🟡 intermediate · 🔴 advanced

## Start here

### Bug Bounty Bootcamp 🟢
**Vickie Li** · No Starch Press · 2021 · [Buy](https://nostarch.com/bug-bounty-bootcamp)

The most complete modern introduction to bug bounty hunting. Covers setting up your environment, recon and every major bug class (XSS, IDOR, SSRF, SSTI, deserialization and more), with chapters on writing reports that get paid.
*Read it if:* you are new to bug bounty and want one book that takes you from zero to submitting reports.

### Real-World Bug Hunting 🟢
**Peter Yaworski** · No Starch Press · 2019 · [Buy](https://nostarch.com/bughunting)

Each chapter explains one vulnerability class through real, paid HackerOne reports. It is the expanded, professionally edited successor to *Web Hacking 101*.
*Read it if:* you learn best from real examples of what actually got paid.

### Web Hacking 101 🟢
**Peter Yaworski** · Leanpub · [Buy](https://leanpub.com/web-hacking-101)

The original self-published version of the book above, sold pay-what-you-want. Prefer *Real-World Bug Hunting* unless you want the cheaper, shorter take.

## Go deeper

### Hacking APIs 🟡
**Corey J. Ball** · No Starch Press · 2022 · [Buy](https://nostarch.com/hacking-apis)

A hands-on guide to REST and GraphQL API testing: discovery, authentication attacks, BOLA, mass assignment, fuzzing and rate-limit bypasses, using deliberately vulnerable labs like crAPI.
*Read it if:* most of your targets are mobile apps or single-page apps backed by APIs (they usually are).

### Bug Bounty Playbook 🟡
**Alex Thomas (ghostlulz)** · self-published

Heavy on recon and automation: subdomain enumeration, asset discovery and tooling pipelines. Sold directly by the author; buy it from the author's official store.
*Read it if:* you already know the bug classes and want to widen your attack surface.

## Classics

### The Web Application Hacker's Handbook, 2nd edition 🔴
**Dafydd Stuttard, Marcus Pinto** · Wiley · 2011 · [Buy](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Web+Application+Hacker%27s+Handbook%3A+Finding+and+Exploiting+Security+Flaws%2C+2nd+Edition-p-9781118026472)

The book that defined web app pentesting methodology. Some chapters are dated, but the thinking behind it holds up. Its authors now maintain the free [Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security), which is effectively the modern edition.
*Read it if:* you want the deep, systematic foundation. Pair it with the Academy labs.

### The Tangled Web 🔴
**Michal Zalewski** · No Starch Press · 2011 · [Buy](https://nostarch.com/tangledweb)

Explains how browsers actually work: URL parsing, the same-origin policy, content sniffing, frames and the quirks that cause whole classes of bugs.
*Read it if:* you want to understand *why* XSS, CSRF and clickjacking exist, not just how to exploit them.
